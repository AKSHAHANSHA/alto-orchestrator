## Who we work with

Alto Motors partners with six UAE banks for auto finance:

- **Emirates NBD**
- **Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank (ADIB)**
- **Dubai Islamic Bank (DIB)**
- **Commercial Bank of Dubai (CBD)**
- **Ajman Bank**
- Plus a rotating panel of other banks the finance team can approach on
  request.

We do not favour one bank over another. The finance consultant will
present offers from at least three of the six for any customer applying,
and the customer decides which to accept.

**The specific terms — profit rates, tenures, fees — live in each bank's
Key Facts Statement**, which is indexed in the platform's knowledge base.
The AI assistant retrieves those documents directly and cites them by
name. This document does not repeat those numbers, because a document that
repeats a number in two places is a document that goes out of date twice.

## Salary transfer

Every one of our partner banks offers a **better profit rate when the
customer's salary is transferred to the financing bank.** The typical
improvement is 100 to 200 basis points — a meaningful reduction on a
five-year instalment.

If a customer is not planning to transfer their salary, we say so
honestly: the rate will be higher and the assistant quotes the "no salary
transfer" band from the finance policy.

Salary transfer is not a hidden condition — it is disclosed at the first
mention of a rate. Customers already banking with one of our six partners
usually find the transfer requirement is already satisfied.

## Regulatory limits

The **Central Bank of the UAE** sets the outer bounds for auto finance:

- **Maximum tenure: 60 months.** No bank in our panel offers a longer
  term regardless of down payment or rate.
- **Maximum loan-to-value: 80%.** The customer must contribute at least a
  **20% down payment** of the vehicle's value.
- **Interest method: reducing balance.** Flat-rate quotes are prohibited
  for consumer auto loans in the UAE.

These are floors, not preferences. If a customer asks for a 72-month
tenure, the assistant explains that no bank in our panel can offer that
under the current regulation.

The CBUAE document setting these rules is indexed in the knowledge base
and is cited when a customer asks *why* they cannot borrow more or for
longer.

## What our finance consultants do

A finance consultant does three things a customer cannot easily do
themselves:

1. **Match the applicant to a lender.** Different banks weigh salary,
   employer, nationality and residency differently. A consultant knows
   which bank is likely to approve a given profile quickly.
2. **Present three offers side by side.** Instalment, total profit,
   early-settlement fee, insurance-bundle terms. The consultant does not
   pick — the customer does.
3. **Handle the paperwork** in one appointment. Salary certificate, bank
   statements, employment letter, cheques for the down payment and the
   security cheque — all of this happens at one desk.

## Eligibility, briefly

Precise eligibility rules vary by bank. General expectations:

- **Age at loan maturity**: 65 for UAE nationals, 60 for expatriates —
  this is a hard limit and reduces the maximum tenure for older customers.
- **Minimum monthly income**: typically AED 8,000 (Karva purchases) or AED
  15,000 (Renzo purchases). Islamic banks are sometimes willing to look at
  slightly lower incomes; conventional banks are stricter.
- **Length of service with current employer**: usually a minimum of six
  months, twelve months for some banks.
- **Debt burden ratio**: total monthly debt service including the new
  instalment must remain under 50% of income (a CBUAE-imposed cap).

If a customer's profile appears to fall outside these bounds the assistant
does not decline — approval is the bank's decision, not ours. The
assistant flags that the profile is at the edge and routes to a finance
consultant.

## Islamic finance

Two of our partner banks (ADIB and DIB) offer Sharia-compliant financing
under Ijara or Murabaha structures. From the customer's perspective the
monthly outgoing is functionally similar — the reducing-balance profit
rate applies the same way — but the underlying legal structure and the
early-settlement mechanics differ.

Customers asking specifically for Islamic finance are routed to a
consultant who works with both Islamic banks. The assistant does not
compare Islamic and conventional finance on cost — that is a decision the
customer makes on faith, not on rate.

## Early settlement

Each bank sets its own early-settlement fee, disclosed in the Key Facts
Statement. The typical range is **1% of the remaining balance**, capped
at AED 10,000 by CBUAE regulation.

Some banks **waive the early-settlement fee for armed-forces personnel**;
this is disclosed in the individual bank documents (see DIB's terms in the
knowledge base for an example).

The assistant does not quote a specific early-settlement figure without
citing the bank whose terms it comes from.

## Insurance

Auto insurance is mandatory for any vehicle sold on finance. Options:

1. **Bank-bundled insurance**: the financing bank offers a first-year
   comprehensive policy at a preferential rate. Convenient, and the cost
   is usually rolled into the finance amount.
2. **Customer's own insurance**: the customer arranges their own policy,
   presents proof at delivery. Often cheaper for customers with a good
   claims history and existing broker relationships.

The assistant does not recommend one over the other — the customer's
existing insurance situation determines which is better.

## What the AI will never do

The assistant on this platform:

- Will **never quote a monthly instalment on an unnamed vehicle.** The
  price is a required input.
- Will **never claim a specific bank will approve a customer.** Approval
  is the bank's decision.
- Will **never quote a rate not sourced from an indexed bank document.**
  The bank documents are the source of truth for numbers.
- Will always attach the disclaimer: *"This is an indicative estimate
  based on published terms and is not a finance offer. Final rates and
  approval are subject to bank assessment."*
